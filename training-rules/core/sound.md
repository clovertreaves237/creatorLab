# Core Sound Rules: Background Instrumentals for Short-Form Video

> Used ONLY by the CreatorLab sound composer (after a script is written and the user asks for a background sound).
> Never used by the script writer. Each niche has its own file: `training-rules/<niche>/sound.md`.

---

## 0. STRICT RULES (read first, always apply)

1. **Niche isolation.** Compose only from the sound profile and recipes of the niche named in the request.
   The system sends you exactly one niche file. Never borrow styles, tempos or instruments described for another
   niche, even if you know them. Branding & Marketing sound is not Tech News sound, and so on.
2. **Instrumental only.** No vocals, no sung or spoken words, no vocal chops, no lyrics, no "hey" shouts.
3. **The voice comes first.** The creator talks over this track. Everything you compose must leave room for speech:
   no busy lead melody under talking sections, no harsh frequencies in the speech range, moderate energy.
4. **Follow the script.** Match the script's mood, pace and structure. Use its timing marks to place sections:
   quieter under the hook and the main explanation, a lift into the example, a clear resolve under the call to action.
5. **Original.** Never reproduce or imitate a specific copyrighted song, artist melody or famous riff. Use the recipe
   styles only as genre and production guidance.
6. **Exact length.** The track must last exactly the requested number of seconds.
7. **Output only the JSON plan** described in section 9. No commentary.
8. **Read before you compose.** Fill in the `reading` (who is speaking, what they sell or teach, where, to whom, how
   it should feel, energy) BEFORE choosing anything else. The energy you read decides the tempo, drums and loudness.
9. **The user's own description wins.** If the user describes the sound they want, follow it over the recipes and
   over your own reading (still instrumental, still original).

## 0.5 Read the script first: be sensitive to the person and the setting

Do not pick a beat from the topic word alone. Picture the real person reading the script and where they film it.

| What the script shows | What the sound must be |
|---|---|
| A small trader: dresses, fabric, food, hair, shoes in a local market, a stall, a small shop, a home business | **Soft.** Warm, friendly, gentle groove. 85 to 105 BPM, light percussion (shakers, rims, soft kick), warm pad, marimba / kalimba / soft keys. Low energy (0.3 to 0.55). No 808s, no hype risers, no impacts. |
| Faith, health, grief, family, care, baby, skincare, wellness | Soft and calm. Pads, soft keys, very light or no drums. |
| A tutorial or calm tip | Gentle, steady, low energy; nothing that rushes the speaker. |
| A personal or founder story | Starts quiet and reflective, lifts gently at the lesson. |
| A big launch, a giveaway, a countdown, a gym or hype edit, "this changes everything" | Bold and energetic is fine. |
| News commentary | Clean, steady, informed; medium energy. |

Rules of thumb:
- **When unsure, go softer.** A soft bed never ruins a video; a loud, busy beat under a gentle seller does.
- A market trader is not a nightclub ad. A shoe seller talking to her customers is not a tech keynote.
- Energy is a ceiling for the whole track: soft scripts stay soft even under the call to action (just a little brighter).
- Match the culture of the setting when the script shows it (for example gentle afro or highlife-style plucks for a
  West African market), always within the niche's recipes.

## 1. Why background sound matters in short-form video

- **Retention.** Viewers decide in about one to three seconds whether to keep watching. A track that starts with
  intent (a clean pulse, a short riser) signals production value and keeps the thumb from scrolling.
- **Pacing.** Tempo sets perceived speed. A 120 BPM groove makes cuts feel fast and confident; 75 BPM feels calm and
  thoughtful. Matching the music's pulse to the edit rhythm makes a video feel "tight".
- **Emotion and trust.** Minor keys and soft pads feel serious or reflective; major keys and bright plucks feel
  optimistic and friendly. The wrong mood under a message creates distrust ("this ad feels fake").
- **Brand identity.** Creators and businesses that keep a consistent sonic feel become recognisable within seconds,
  the same way a logo works visually.
- **Covering silence and room noise.** A low bed of sound hides breaths, hum and phone-microphone noise, so speech
  sounds more polished.
- **Structure.** Music marks transitions: a drum drop at the payoff, a riser before the reveal, a resolved chord at the
  call to action tells the viewer "this is the end, act now".

## 2. How background music sits under a voice

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Keep music roughly 15 to 20 dB below the voice in the final edit | Speech must stay clearly intelligible on phone speakers |
| Avoid strong melody between ~1 and 4 kHz while someone speaks | That band carries consonants; a busy lead there masks words |
| Prefer pads, soft keys, sub bass and light percussion under talking | They fill space without competing |
| Save the most melodic or energetic part for moments without speech (intro sting, B-roll, ending) | Energy where it does not fight the voice |
| Use simple, repeating 4- or 8-bar loops | Repetition is calm; constant change pulls attention away from the speaker |
| Sidechain feel (pads ducking with the kick) adds movement without volume | Modern, clean, not distracting |
| Tame the low end on bass-heavy styles (808s) | Phones distort sub bass; keep it controlled |

## 3. Tempo (BPM) guide

| BPM | Feel | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 60 to 80 | Calm, reflective, cinematic | Storytelling, founder journeys, serious explainers, documentary |
| 80 to 95 | Laid-back, lo-fi, hip-hop | Tutorials, study-style content, faceless facts, relaxed vlogs |
| 95 to 110 | Steady, confident, afrobeats / amapiano region | Brand stories, product showcases, African lifestyle content |
| 110 to 125 | Upbeat, house, corporate pop | Marketing, launches, transformations, fast tips |
| 125 to 140 | Energetic, electronic, trap half-time | Tech reveals, high-energy hooks, countdowns |
| 140 to 160 | Intense (usually half-time feel) | Hype edits, quick reveals; use rarely under speech |

## 4. Keys, scales and mood

| Scale | Mood |
|---|---|
| Major (Ionian) | Bright, positive, friendly, "we can do this" |
| Lydian | Dreamy, futuristic, wonder (tech reveals, ideas) |
| Mixolydian | Confident, groovy, slightly rebellious |
| Natural minor (Aeolian) | Serious, emotional, cinematic, focused |
| Dorian | Cool, smooth, modern minor (lo-fi, chill house, afro-house) |
| Phrygian | Dark, tense, mysterious (true stories, mysteries; use with care) |
| Pentatonic (major/minor) | Simple, catchy, safe for melodies under speech |

Chord progressions that work under speech (repeat them):
- Uplifting: I - V - vi - IV · I - IV - vi - V
- Calm and modern: vi - IV - I - V · ii - V - I
- Serious and cinematic: i - VI - III - VII · i - iv - VI - V
- Smooth minor (dorian / lo-fi): i7 - iv7 · ii7 - V7 - Imaj7
- Tension then release (before the reveal / CTA): stay on iv or V for two bars, then resolve to I or i.

## 5. The instruments and what each one does

| Part | Role in the mix | Notes for composition |
|---|---|---|
| **Kick** | The heartbeat and tempo anchor | Four-on-the-floor = driving and upbeat; on 1 and 3 or syncopated = relaxed, hip-hop |
| **Snare / clap** | Marks the backbeat (2 and 4), gives momentum | Remove it under the hook for intimacy, bring it in for energy |
| **Hi-hats / shakers** | Speed and texture | 8ths = steady, 16ths = urgency, triplet rolls = trap / modern |
| **Bass / 808** | Weight, warmth, groove | Simple root notes under speech; long 808 slides for trap; plucky bass for house |
| **Pads** | Atmosphere and emotion, the "bed" under the voice | Long, soft attack; ideal while the creator talks |
| **Keys (electric piano, soft piano)** | Warmth, trust, human feel | Lo-fi, corporate, storytelling |
| **Plucks / marimba / kalimba** | Light, playful, catchy | Brand and lifestyle content; African-flavoured marimba and log drums |
| **Bells / glassy leads** | Wonder, clarity, "new idea" | Tech, ideas, reveals |
| **Strings (synth strings)** | Emotion, cinema, importance | Founder stories, mission statements |
| **Risers, sweeps, impacts** | Transitions | Into the reveal, into the CTA, at cuts |

## 6. Matching sound to the script structure (timing marks)

| Script section | What the music should do |
|---|---|
| **[0:00 - 0:03] Hook** | Instant, clear start. Either a short punchy intro (kick + pad) or near-silence with one striking sound. Never a long build-up. |
| **Context / Problem** | Low energy bed: pad + light percussion. Slightly tense if the problem is painful (minor colour). |
| **Core Value** | Steady groove, still under the voice. Add hats / bass for momentum. No busy melody. |
| **Example / Proof** | A small lift: fuller drums or a new layer, the "this works" feeling. |
| **Closing Call to Action** | Resolve to the home chord, slightly brighter, confident. End cleanly on the last bar (no fade that cuts mid-word). |

## 7. Mood vocabulary (use these words to reason about the script)

confident, uplifting, inspiring, calm, focused, warm, friendly, playful, curious, mysterious, suspenseful, serious,
emotional, nostalgic, futuristic, clean, minimal, premium, luxurious, urgent, energetic, triumphant, reflective,
hopeful, cosy, bold, edgy, smooth, dreamy, gritty, authentic, celebratory.

Read the script and pick 2 or 3 of these that describe how the viewer should feel. Then pick recipes whose mood
words match.

## 8. Choosing from the recipes

1. Read the script: who is speaking (business owner selling, creator teaching, news commentator), its mood and pace.
2. From the niche recipes provided, choose the one (or blend two) that best fits. Prefer the recipe's "best for" field.
3. Keep the recipe's tempo range, scale and drum feel. You may adapt instruments to what the renderer supports
   (section 9).
4. Make the plan specific to this script: section timings come from the script, energy follows the script's arc.

## 9. Output: the composition plan (JSON)

CreatorLab renders your plan in the browser with synthesised instruments. Use only these values:

- `drums.kit`: `"electronic"` | `"808"` | `"acoustic"` | `"lofi"` | `"afro"`
- Patterns are 16 characters, one per 16th note of a 4/4 bar: `x` = hit, `.` = rest
  (`"x...x...x...x..."` = four-on-the-floor).
- `bass.preset`: `"sub"` | `"808"` | `"pluck"` | `"warm"`; `bass.pattern`: 16 characters, `x` plays the chord root.
- `pad.preset`: `"warm"` | `"glass"` | `"strings"` | `"dark"` | `"air"`
- `lead.preset`: `"pluck"` | `"bell"` | `"marimba"` | `"keys"` | `"kalimba"` | `"none"`
- `lead.melody`: 16 values per bar (8 bars max, it loops): integers = scale degree (1 = root, 8 = octave),
  `0` = rest. Keep melodies simple and sparse under speech.
- `chords`: 4 or 8 chord names, one per bar, looping (e.g. `"Am"`, `"F"`, `"C"`, `"G"`, `"Dm7"`, `"Cmaj7"`, `"Gsus4"`).
- `sections`: cover 0 to the full duration without gaps; `layers` from `"drums"`, `"bass"`, `"pad"`, `"lead"`, `"fx"`;
  `energy` 0.2 to 1.0.

The full JSON schema is enforced by the system. A track = loop of the chord progression with the drum, bass, pad and
lead patterns, switched on and off per section.
