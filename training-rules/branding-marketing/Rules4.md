# 500 branding and marketing format executions

The format executions below are derived from the content forms and marketing practices in the transcripts. They combine 50 core formats with 10 story angles. Each execution includes two short-story examples, for 500 format executions and 1,000 examples total.

## 50 core formats

1. **Short-form vertical problem–solution video** — A fast social video that opens with a recognizable need and shows a useful response.
2. **FAQ or objection-response clip** — A direct answer to one real customer question or purchase hesitation.
3. **Step-by-step tutorial** — A practical walkthrough for one task or use case.
4. **Educational carousel** — A swipeable lesson that moves from question to explanation and action.
5. **Static story-led ad** — A single image or graphic that carries a tension, value, and proof point.
6. **Customer testimonial interview** — A real customer describes a need, decision, and experience.
7. **Before-and-after sequence** — A documented change shown with context about what changed.
8. **Customer case study** — A deeper narrative about context, choice, implementation, and verified results.
9. **Review or quote creative** — An approved review or quote presented with enough context to interpret it.
10. **Customer journey timeline** — A visual map from first need through decision, use, and follow-up.
11. **Founder-origin story** — The observation or problem that led to creating an offer.
12. **Expert explainer** — A knowledgeable person makes a complex idea clear and useful.
13. **Founder or employee Q&A** — A direct question reveals a decision, value, or lesson from the team.
14. **Podcast or interview clip** — A longer conversation edited around one memorable insight or story.
15. **About-page or brand-story article** — A written narrative explaining purpose, choices, values, and audience.
16. **Product demonstration** — The product is shown in use so people can understand how it works.
17. **Unboxing or reveal video** — A first look at a product or package, emphasizing discovery and details.
18. **Behind-the-scenes process video** — A production, preparation, or service step is made visible.
19. **Product-detail or macro showcase** — Close views of material, construction, texture, or function.
20. **Product-page narrative** — A product page answers what it is, who it suits, and why it is valuable.
21. **Street interview** — Short public questions surface needs, opinions, or reactions.
22. **Employee-generated story** — A staff member shares a real work moment, customer question, or insight.
23. **UGC creator video** — Creator-led content explains a product or service in a native, personal style.
24. **Creator partnership or advocacy series** — Repeated creator appearances build familiarity and sustained trust.
25. **Community event recap** — A gathering is told through people, moments, learning, and shared experience.
26. **High-production campaign film** — A polished visual story uses deliberate setting, pacing, and art direction.
27. **Low-fi native social clip** — Informal direct-to-camera content that feels natural in a social feed.
28. **Day-in-the-life vlog** — A routine or workday reveals decisions, process, and point of view.
29. **Recurring branded series** — A repeatable episode structure makes the content recognizable over time.
30. **Social show or episodic format** — A recurring show uses a host, guests, questions, or audience participation.
31. **Fair product comparison** — Options are compared by use case, features, tradeoffs, and customer needs.
32. **Myth-busting video** — A common assumption is examined with explanation and evidence.
33. **Social-proof montage** — Authentic comments, use cases, or endorsements are grouped around a need.
34. **Alternative breakdown guide** — A guide compares different approaches and when each one makes sense.
35. **Live demonstration or stream** — A real-time walkthrough answers audience questions as they arise.
36. **Launch countdown or reveal** — A staged sequence introduces a launch through clues, details, and reveal.
37. **Seasonal or cultural-moment campaign** — A timely story connects the offer to a relevant season or shared moment.
38. **Challenge or contest activation** — An audience prompt invites participation around a relevant task.
39. **Product-sampling story** — A sample is introduced in a relevant setting and followed by honest reactions.
40. **Brand-collaboration story** — Two brands or disciplines come together around a shared audience or idea.
41. **Storytime vignette** — A compact personal or customer-inspired narrative has a clear turn.
42. **Green-screen commentary** — A speaker reacts to a relevant image, claim, or trend with added context.
43. **One-shot narrative video** — A continuous visual sequence follows a single action or reveal.
44. **Situational skit** — A relatable scene dramatizes a common misunderstanding or friction.
45. **Template or trend adaptation** — A familiar format is adapted to express a genuine brand or product difference.
46. **Welcome or onboarding email** — A first message helps a new customer understand what to expect and do next.
47. **Promotional email or offer narrative** — An offer is presented through a customer need, clear value, and simple terms.
48. **Newsletter or editorial recommendation** — A useful recommendation is explained through a point of view and relevant context.
49. **Landing-page case narrative** — A page moves from customer context to solution, evidence, and action.
50. **Sales-enablement customer-proof deck** — A concise story helps a buyer explain a decision and evidence internally.

## Story angles

1. **Problem-to-solution** — Frame the story around a real friction, a practical response, and a clear next step.
2. **Origin and purpose** — Show the observation or need that sparked the brand, product, or campaign.
3. **Mechanism and how it works** — Explain the steps, design, or service process that makes the offer useful.
4. **Objection and proof** — Start with a reasonable concern and answer it with relevant, verified evidence.
5. **Comparison and tradeoff** — Help the audience compare options against consistent criteria and use cases.
6. **Transformation and journey** — Follow a before, turning point, and documented change without exaggerating outcomes.
7. **Craft and detail** — Reveal the design, materials, decisions, or process behind the finished experience.
8. **Identity and community** — Connect the story to shared values, routines, or a community people can recognize.
9. **Launch, offer, and timing** — Build anticipation around a relevant launch or offer while keeping terms and timing clear.
10. **Feedback and iteration** — Show how a question, test, or response changed the next version and what was learned.

Examples are adaptable story prompts, not claims about real customers. Use verified details and customer permission for testimonials, reviews, endorsements, results, and before-and-after content.

# Format executions and examples

## Core format 1: Short-form vertical problem–solution video

### 001. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Problem-to-solution

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Frame the story around a real friction, a practical response, and a clear next step.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A first-time shopper considering an everyday apparel item faces uncertainty about which option suits them; the story uses a one-page comparison guide to make one practical next step clear.
2. A busy parent considering a creative service faces a repeated manual task; the story uses a transparent pricing breakdown to make one practical next step clear.

### 002. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Origin and purpose

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Show the observation or need that sparked the brand, product, or campaign.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A team hears about a page that hides key details from a new team member while they consider a personal-care product; that observation inspires a return-policy summary and clarifies why the offer exists.
2. A team hears about a crowded shelf with no comparison cues from a community organizer while they consider a retail display; that observation inspires a side-by-side use case and clarifies why the offer exists.

### 003. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Mechanism and how it works

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Explain the steps, design, or service process that makes the offer useful.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A product designer wants to select a suitable product version; a demonstration of a small-business software tool uses a visual label to show how the process works and where it may fit.
2. A prospective client wants to prepare for a launch; a demonstration of an onboarding program uses a close-up product detail to show how the process works and where it may fit.

### 004. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Objection and proof

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Start with a reasonable concern and answer it with relevant, verified evidence.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A subscription member questions whether a crafted home object fits their needs; evidence such as a transparent demonstration under stated conditions provides context and makes the limits of the claim clear.
2. A small-business owner questions whether an everyday apparel item fits their needs; evidence such as a case example shared with permission provides context and makes the limits of the claim clear.

### 005. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Comparison and tradeoff

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Help the audience compare options against consistent criteria and use cases.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A first-time shopper compares a fitness accessory with a different feature set; a guide to repair and care helps explain which option is better suited to a daily commute.
2. A busy parent compares a personal-care product with a quick fix; a guide to the context where each works best helps explain which option is better suited to travel.

### 006. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Transformation and journey

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Follow a before, turning point, and documented change without exaggerating outcomes.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A new team member starts with a content plan that changes every week; the story follows how clear care instructions change one step in their routine, with any result shown only if verified.
2. A community organizer starts with a search that returns too many choices; the story follows how a sample or trial changes one step in their routine, with any result shown only if verified.

### 007. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Craft and detail

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Reveal the design, materials, decisions, or process behind the finished experience.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A designer shows one detail of a community membership; the maker uses a live demo to reveal the decisions behind the finish and addresses a price they cannot evaluate.
2. A service lead shows one detail of a crafted home object; the maker uses a short demonstration to reveal the decisions behind the finish and addresses a maintenance step they keep forgetting.

### 008. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Identity and community

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Connect the story to shared values, routines, or a community people can recognize.

**Short-story examples:**

1. In a community of returning customers, a subscription member shares a repeated manual task; the group uses checklist to turn a solo question into a practical conversation.
2. In a community of people comparing products, a small-business owner shares a purchase requiring multiple approvals; a community Q&A turns a solo question into a practical conversation.

### 009. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Launch, offer, and timing

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Build anticipation around a relevant launch or offer while keeping terms and timing clear.

**Short-story examples:**

1. Before a specialty food item is introduced, the team previews a real customer question; a first-time shopper gets a clear answer to a crowded shelf with no comparison cues and knows what to expect.
2. Before a customer-support service is introduced, the team previews an expert walkthrough; a busy parent gets a clear answer to conflicting advice and knows what to expect.

### 010. Short-form vertical problem–solution video × Feedback and iteration

**Execution:** Hook visually in the opening seconds; show the friction, the product or process, and one takeaway. Show how a question, test, or response changed the next version and what was learned.

**Short-story examples:**

1. After a live-event question from a new team member, the team revisits a learning program; the team uses a step-by-step sequence to show what changed and what the team learned.
2. After a recurring customer objection from a community organizer, the team revisits a community membership; the team uses a one-page comparison guide to show what changed and what the team learned.

**Execution:** Keep a familiar opening and structure while giving each episode a fresh lesson. Frame the story around a real friction, a practical response, and a clear next step.

**Short-story examples:**

1. An independent creator considering a premium product faces a service process that feels opaque; the story uses a one-page comparison guide to make one practical next step clear.
2. A workshop maker considering a learning program faces a launch message that assumes too much; the story uses a transparent pricing breakdown to make one practical next step clear.

### 282. Recurring branded series × Origin and purpose

**Execution:** Keep a familiar opening and structure while giving each episode a fresh lesson. Show the observation or need that sparked the brand, product, or campaign.

**Short-story examples:**

1. A team hears about a content plan that changes every week from a student while they consider a seasonal product; that observation inspires a return-policy summary and clarifies why the offer exists.
2. A team hears about a search that returns too many choices from a local shop owner while they consider a subscription offer; that observation inspires a side-by-side use case and clarifies why the offer exists.

**Execution:** Keep a familiar opening and structure while giving each episode a fresh lesson. Explain the steps, design, or service process that makes the offer useful.

### 284. Recurring branded series × Objection and proof

**Short-story examples:**

### 285. Recurring branded series × Comparison and tradeoff

**Short-story examples:**

**Short-story examples:**
