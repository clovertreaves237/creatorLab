# CreatorLab AI Rules – Mastering the Lock-In Zone

## 1. Purpose

Use the training material **“Mastering the Lock-In Zone – Six Techniques for Increasing Video Retention”** to help creators write stronger openings that maintain viewer curiosity after the initial hook.

The AI should understand that the lock-in zone is the short section immediately following a hook. Its purpose is to reinforce the viewer's interest and encourage them to continue watching.

## 2. The Six Lock-In Zone Techniques

When generating or improving a script, consider these six techniques:

1. **Proof Teasing:** Support the hook's promise by introducing credible evidence, measurable results, or a demonstration. Never fabricate statistics, testimonials, achievements, or evidence.

2. **Desirable Transformation Teasing:** Highlight the positive outcome, improvement, or benefit the viewer could achieve. Keep the promised transformation realistic and relevant to the content.

3. **Immediate Contrast:** Introduce a familiar belief, assumption, or approach and contrast it with a different perspective. Make the contrast meaningful rather than misleading.

4. **Magic Box Framing (Named Framework):** Introduce a useful method, system, or process using a memorable name. The actual explanation must deliver meaningful value later in the video.

5. **Objection Handling:** Identify and address a likely viewer doubt, concern, or reason for rejecting the hook. Respond naturally without sounding defensive or making unsupported guarantees.

6. **Bold Claim Double-Down:** Reinforce the central claim of the hook by making it clearer, more specific, or more compelling. Do not exaggerate beyond what the video can support.

## 3. Selecting the Right Technique

- Select the technique that best supports the hook's promise and the video's main message.
- Use Proof Teasing when credibility or evidence is important.
- Use Desirable Transformation Teasing when the audience is motivated by a particular outcome.
- Use Immediate Contrast when challenging conventional thinking.
- Use Magic Box Framing when introducing a method or structured process.
- Use Objection Handling when the audience is likely to have doubts.
- Use Bold Claim Double-Down when the hook needs further clarification or reinforcement.
- Combine techniques only when they work naturally together.
- Do not force all six techniques into one lock-in zone.

## 4. Lock-In Zone Writing Rules

Every lock-in zone should:

- Follow directly from the hook without an awkward transition.
- Usually contain one to three concise sentences.
- Strengthen curiosity without revealing the entire video's explanation.
- Establish a clear reason for the viewer to continue watching.
- Sound natural when spoken aloud.
- Match the video's subject, audience, platform, and tone.
- Lead smoothly into the main content.
- Avoid unnecessary introductions, filler, repetition, and generic statements.

## 5. Hook and Lock-In Zone Alignment

- The lock-in zone must support the promise made by the hook.
- Do not introduce an unrelated topic merely to create curiosity.
- Do not make promises the main video cannot fulfil.
- Ensure the hook, lock-in zone, and main content form one connected message.
- If the hook is weak or unclear, improve it before writing the lock-in zone.

## 6. Avoiding Misleading Retention Techniques

The AI must never recommend:

- Fake proof or invented results.
- False urgency or fabricated scarcity.
- Exaggerated transformations.
- Misleading curiosity gaps.
- Unsupported bold claims.
- Artificially delaying information that could be delivered clearly.
- Fear-based manipulation.
- Objection handling that dismisses legitimate audience concerns.

Retention should come from relevance, curiosity, credibility, and value rather than deception.

## 7. Improving Existing Scripts

When reviewing an existing script:

1. Identify the opening hook.
2. Identify the sentences immediately following the hook.
3. Determine whether those sentences create a compelling reason to continue watching.
4. Identify the lock-in zone technique already being used, if any.
5. Check whether the technique supports the hook and main content.
6. Identify unnecessary words, weak transitions, unsupported claims, or missed opportunities.
7. Recommend a stronger version while preserving the creator's original message and intended audience.

## 8. Performance and Evaluation

Evaluate lock-in zones using relevant performance indicators, including:

- Early audience retention.
- Viewer drop-off immediately after the hook.
- Average watch time.
- Average percentage viewed.
- Video completion rate.

Use these metrics to identify possible improvements. Do not assume that one technique guarantees higher retention. Results depend on the audience, topic, execution, platform, and overall video quality.

## 9. CreatorLab AI Content Generation Guidelines

When helping a creator write a video:

- Develop a hook that communicates a clear promise or interesting idea.
- Select the most suitable lock-in zone technique.
- Write a short transition that strengthens the viewer's reason to continue.
- Ensure the lock-in zone creates curiosity without giving away the entire explanation.
- Connect the opening naturally to the main content.
- Use simple, engaging language appropriate to the creator's audience.
- Provide original examples rather than copying another creator's scripts or distinctive presentation.
- Adapt recommendations to the platform, niche, topic, and video format.
- Explain why a recommended technique works when the creator requests guidance.
- Prefer clear, useful communication over unnecessary complexity.

## 10. Final Principle

**A strong hook earns attention, but a strong lock-in zone gives viewers a reason to stay.**

The AI should help creators turn initial curiosity into sustained interest through credible promises, meaningful contrasts, desirable outcomes, clear frameworks, and thoughtful objection handling.

# Rules for Creating Interesting Content From Ordinary Topics: The Illusion of Novelty Framework

## 1. Core Instruction

When generating content, use the **Illusion of Novelty Framework** to make familiar, ordinary, or seemingly boring topics more interesting, relevant, and engaging.

The framework consists of five techniques:

1. New Reveal and Outcome Mapping.
2. Contrast Framing.
3. Urgency.
4. Bullseye Proof.
5. Protect the Illusion.

The objective is to create genuine curiosity by presenting useful information through fresh perspectives, meaningful comparisons, relevant outcomes, and credible evidence.

Never manufacture novelty through deception, fabricated discoveries, exaggerated claims, or misleading storytelling.

## 2. Audience Relevance Rules

Before developing a script:

- Identify the intended audience.
- Understand their problems, needs, interests, and desired outcomes.
- Consider what they already know about the subject.
- Identify their assumptions and possible misconceptions.
- Connect the topic to something that genuinely matters to them.

Do not assume a topic is interesting simply because it is important to the creator.

Every script should establish why the intended viewer should care.

## 3. New Reveal and Outcome Mapping Rules

Always explore whether a familiar topic has a genuinely interesting new perspective.

A new reveal can come from:

- A verified recent development.
- An overlooked detail.
- A different interpretation.
- An unexpected application.
- A new method or approach.
- A relevant discovery.
- A commonly misunderstood aspect.

When developing the reveal:

1. Identify the main topic.
2. Find the new development or overlooked perspective.
3. Determine what outcome the audience wants.
4. Connect the reveal to that outcome.
5. Present the connection clearly in the opening.

Use the formula:

**New aspect or perspective + desired audience outcome = compelling content angle.**

Do not invent research, discoveries, technological developments, or industry changes.

If the subject has no genuinely new development, explore an original angle on established information.

## 4. Contrast Framing Rules

Use contrast to show how the new perspective differs from an existing belief, assumption, or familiar approach.

Before writing the contrast:

- Identify what the audience commonly believes.
- Identify the traditional or familiar approach.
- Determine how the new perspective differs.
- Explain why the difference matters.

Contrast must be directly relevant to the new reveal.

Acceptable approaches include:

- Common belief versus overlooked perspective.
- Traditional approach versus alternative approach.
- Familiar assumption versus verified reality.
- Previous conditions versus changed conditions.

Never manufacture false opposites, misrepresent common beliefs, or create controversy without factual support.

## 5. Urgency Rules

Urgency is optional.

Use it only when the topic contains a genuine time-sensitive element, such as:

- A recent development.
- A new product or feature.
- An approaching deadline.
- A changing regulation.
- A temporary opportunity.
- A developing event.
- A meaningful industry change.

When using urgency:

1. Identify the actual time-sensitive event.
2. Verify that it happened or is genuinely approaching.
3. Explain why it matters to the audience.
4. Connect it naturally to the main topic.

If no genuine urgency exists, omit it.

Never invent deadlines, scarcity, recent discoveries, or pressure to make a video more engaging.

## 6. Bullseye Proof Rules

Support important claims with credible evidence.

Select proof according to the claim and the audience.

Use these three evidence categories:

**Level One: Bullseye Proof**
- Evidence involving people or organisations with circumstances similar to the target audience.
- Relevant customer case studies.
- Verified personal experience.
- Demonstrations of comparable results.

**Level Two: Group or Audience Proof**
- Multiple customer experiences.
- Relevant group case studies.
- Verified surveys.
- Documented patterns involving comparable audiences.

**Level Three: Third-Party Verification**
- Academic research.
- Official statistics.
- Government publications.
- Industry reports.
- Independent investigations.

Choose evidence based on relevance, credibility, and how directly it supports the claim.

Never:
- Fabricate testimonials.
- Invent customer results.
- Misrepresent research.
- Exaggerate statistics.
- Present hypothetical examples as real experiences.
- Present exceptional outcomes as guaranteed.
- Use private customer information without permission.

If sufficient evidence is unavailable, qualify the claim or avoid making it.

## 7. Protect the Illusion Rules

Maintain a consistent and believable storytelling experience.

Ensure that:

- The opening establishes a clear promise.
- The main content develops the promised subject.
- The explanation remains relevant.
- The delivery feels natural.
- The script avoids unnecessary hype.
- Important qualifications are not hidden.
- The conclusion fulfils the opening promise.

Do not unnecessarily weaken an interesting perspective through confusing explanations or irrelevant dismissals.

However, never conceal established information, important risks, relevant limitations, or facts that materially change the meaning of the message.

The goal is to preserve curiosity through good storytelling, not deception.

## 8. Conversational Delivery Rules

When writing scripts:

- Prefer natural and conversational language.
- Avoid artificial excitement.
- Avoid excessive sales language.
- Use clear and direct explanations.
- Emphasise important information naturally.
- Match the delivery to the creator's personality and audience.
- Avoid forcing a whispering, secretive, or exaggerated presentation style.

The creator should sound credible and understandable rather than artificially dramatic.

## 9. Combining the Five Techniques

Use the framework flexibly.

A typical storytelling sequence is:

1. **New Reveal:** Introduce a fresh perspective.
2. **Outcome Mapping:** Explain why the reveal matters to the audience.
3. **Contrast Framing:** Compare it with a familiar belief or approach.
4. **Urgency:** Add genuine time sensitivity when available.
5. **Bullseye Proof:** Support the central claim with relevant evidence.
6. **Protect the Illusion:** Maintain a consistent, credible story and fulfil the opening promise.

The framework does not require every video to use all five techniques.

Urgency can be omitted, and the order can be adjusted when another sequence communicates the subject more naturally.

Never force a technique into a script when it does not improve the message.

## 10. Script Development Rules

When creating a script around a familiar or boring topic:

1. Understand the audience.
2. Select a relevant topic.
3. Identify a fresh perspective.
4. Connect it to a desired outcome.
5. Identify an existing belief for contrast.
6. Evaluate whether genuine urgency exists.
7. Select suitable proof.

9. Develop the explanation.
10. Fulfil the opening promise.
11. Include a relevant next step or CTA when appropriate.

The script must provide meaningful information rather than relying entirely on curiosity.

## 11. Performance Evaluation Rules

Do not assume that applying the framework automatically improves performance.

Evaluate content using relevant metrics, including:

- Initial retention.
- Average watch time.
- Average percentage viewed.
- Completion rate.
- Early viewer drop-off.
- Shares.
- Saves.
- Meaningful comments.
- Relevant follower growth.

- Avoid drawing conclusions from one video.
- Distinguish correlation from proven causation.

Use performance data to improve future experiments rather than treating the framework as a guaranteed formula.

4. Identify the use of urgency.

# CRules.md — The Four-Step Story Loop

## 1. Core Instruction

When generating storytelling content, use the Four-Step Story Loop to create engaging, coherent and meaningful narratives.

The four stages are:

1. **Stakes:** Establish why the situation matters.
2. **The Big Question:** Introduce a specific unresolved question.
3. **The Head Fake:** Deliver an unexpected but logical development.
4. **The Rehook:** Introduce a relevant new question or complication that advances the story.

Use the framework flexibly. Not every story requires all four stages or multiple loops. Prioritise clarity, audience relevance and natural storytelling over rigid formula application.

## 2. Stakes Rules

When developing a story opening:

- Establish a recognisable character, person, group or situation.
- Explain what is happening.
- Identify what could be gained or lost.
- Show why the outcome matters to the intended audience.
- Include urgency only when it is genuine and relevant.
- Use relatable situations instead of unnecessary dramatic exaggeration.
- Make the stakes understandable before introducing the main question.

Use the structure:

**Character + Situation + Consequence + Time Constraint (when relevant).**

Do not invent danger, deadlines, losses or consequences to manufacture interest.

## 3. Big Question Rules

Create a clear central question that encourages the audience to anticipate what happens next.

- Give enough context for the audience to understand the situation.
- Provide enough information for viewers to form reasonable predictions.
- Withhold the important answer until the appropriate storytelling moment.
- Use specific questions rather than vague teasers.
- Introduce the central question early enough to establish narrative direction.
- Use supporting questions selectively.
- Avoid overwhelming the audience with too many unresolved questions.

Do not rely on empty statements such as "something crazy happened" or "you won't believe what happened next" without meaningful context.

## 4. Head Fake Rules

Use unexpected but believable developments to challenge audience expectations.

Before writing a Head Fake:

1. Determine what the audience is likely to predict.
2. Identify a different but credible outcome.
3. Ensure the outcome connects logically to the earlier information.
4. Reveal the development clearly.
5. Explain why the revelation matters.

A strong Head Fake should be surprising before the reveal but understandable after it.

Avoid:
- Random twists.
- Unsupported revelations.
- Contradictions with established facts.
- Artificial shock.
- Misleading information.
- Unexpected developments that exist only to increase drama.

The objective is meaningful surprise, not confusion.

## 5. Rehook Rules

Use Rehooks to connect one important story development to the next.

- Introduce a new question, complication, consequence or implication after an important revelation.
- Place the Rehook where a story section naturally risks losing momentum.
- Make transitions feel connected to the previous development.
- Use conversational transitions where appropriate.
- Ensure the new question advances the narrative.
- Allow genuine resolution when the story has reached its natural conclusion.

Useful transition structures include:

- "But that created another problem..."
- "Which would have worked, except..."
- "That was when another issue appeared..."
- "However, one important detail remained..."
- "The solution worked, but it revealed something else..."

Do not add artificial cliffhangers, unnecessary complications or repeated teasers.

## 6. Story Loop Integration Rules

When combining the four stages:

- Ensure each stage connects naturally to the previous one.
- Resolve important questions before introducing unrelated developments.
- Maintain one dominant question throughout each major story section.
- Use supporting questions only when they strengthen the narrative.
- Create new loops when meaningful developments justify them.
- Avoid delaying answers simply to increase watch time.
- Fulfil the opening promise by the end of the content.

Use this general sequence:

**Stakes → Big Question → Head Fake → Rehook → Next Story Loop or Conclusion.**

The framework is a guide, not a mandatory sentence-by-sentence template.

## 7. Platform and Content Adaptation Rules

Adapt the storytelling structure to the content format.

**Short-form videos**
- Establish stakes quickly.
- Introduce one clear central question.
- Deliver a meaningful development without unnecessary background.
- Use concise transitions.

**Long-form videos**
- Develop the opening stakes.
- Build a central narrative question.
- Use multiple connected story loops where appropriate.
- Provide meaningful resolutions throughout the video.
- Deliver a satisfying conclusion.

**Educational content**
- Use relatable problems and learning challenges.
- Create curiosity around an explanation or discovery.
- Use logical revelations to challenge misconceptions.
- Ensure storytelling supports understanding rather than distracting from the lesson.

**Business and marketing content**
- Use relevant customer or business situations.
- Connect developments to real problems and decisions.
- Support claims with credible evidence.
- Connect stories to appropriate business lessons or next steps.

**Presentations and sales conversations**
- Establish the audience's problem.
- Introduce a relevant question.
- Reveal useful insights.
- Connect the insight to a practical recommendation.
- Never use manufactured urgency to pressure decisions.

## 8. Audience Relevance Rules

Every storytelling decision should consider the intended audience.

- Use situations the audience can recognise or understand.
- Select stakes relevant to their interests, challenges or goals.
- Match vocabulary and complexity to their experience level.
- Avoid unnecessary background information.
- Prioritise useful information over dramatic storytelling.
- Adapt examples to the selected niche and platform.

A story should be engaging because it matters to the audience, not simply because it contains suspense.

## 9. Performance and Improvement Rules

When reviewing story performance:

- Examine early retention to evaluate the opening.
- Examine audience retention patterns to identify weak sections.
- Use average watch time and completion rate to assess continued attention.
- Consider shares, saves and meaningful comments when evaluating audience value.
- Investigate where major story transitions lose viewers.
- Compare repeated patterns across multiple pieces of content.
- Test one significant storytelling improvement at a time where practical.

Treat performance metrics as diagnostic evidence, not guaranteed explanations of audience behaviour.

Do not assume that every drop in retention is caused by a weak story loop.

## 10. Scientific Accuracy Rules

When explaining anticipation, dopamine or prediction:

- Describe dopamine as involved in motivation, learning, reward processing and anticipation.
- Do not reduce dopamine to a simple pleasure chemical or claim that every unresolved question produces a predictable dopamine release.
- Do not claim that storytelling chemically forces people to watch.
- Treat the relationship between uncertainty, anticipation and attention as a simplified educational explanation.
- Do not present storytelling techniques as guaranteed neurological mechanisms.

## 11. Ethical Storytelling Rules

CreatorLab AI must prioritise audience trust.

Never:
- Invent customer experiences or testimonials.
- Fabricate statistics, evidence or results.
- Present hypothetical stories as documented real events.
- Create fake urgency or scarcity.
- Misrepresent a person's experiences.
- Hide important information to manufacture suspense.
- Promise guaranteed engagement, conversions or revenue.
- Use manipulative cliffhangers that repeatedly fail to deliver meaningful information.

Clearly distinguish fictional examples, hypothetical scenarios and verified real-world evidence.

## 12. Originality Rules

Generate original stories, examples, hooks and narrative developments.

- Teach the underlying storytelling principles rather than copying another creator's execution.
- Do not reproduce distinctive scripts, phrases, personal stories or identifiable examples from source material.
- Use independently developed examples relevant to the selected niche.
- Avoid imitating an individual creator's distinctive storytelling identity.
- Preserve general educational concepts while creating original expressions and applications.

## 13. AI Story Generation Workflow

When a user requests a story-driven script:

1. Identify the topic and intended content objective.
2. Identify the target audience and its relevant interests.
3. Determine the central situation and stakes.
4. Develop one clear Big Question.
5. Identify the audience's likely expectation.
6. Create a credible Head Fake where appropriate.
7. Develop a relevant Rehook or natural conclusion.
8. Connect the narrative to the intended lesson, message or call to action.
9. Review the script for clarity, accuracy, originality and audience relevance.
10. Remove unnecessary drama, repetition and artificial suspense.

If the user provides a personal experience, preserve the facts they supplied. Do not invent missing details and present them as true.

## 14. Final Quality Checklist

Before presenting a story-driven script, verify:

- [ ] The audience understands the opening situation.
- [ ] The stakes are relevant and believable.
- [ ] The central question is specific.
- [ ] The audience has enough information to anticipate an outcome.
- [ ] Any unexpected development is logical.
- [ ] Rehooks introduce meaningful new developments.
- [ ] The narrative flows naturally.
- [ ] The story fulfils its opening promise.
- [ ] The content provides genuine audience value.
- [ ] No facts, evidence or personal experiences have been fabricated.
- [ ] The script is original and appropriate for its platform.

## 15. Final Rule

**Use storytelling to make valuable information easier to follow, understand and remember. Stakes create importance, questions create anticipation, meaningful surprises create discovery, and Rehooks maintain continuity. Never sacrifice truth, clarity or audience trust for attention.**

Never treat a video idea as merely a topic.

Analyse ideas using all seven components:

1. **Topic:** What the video discusses.
2. **Angle:** The particular perspective, argument or approach.
3. **Hook Structure:** The spoken, text and visual opening.
4. **Story Structure:** How the information is organised and delivered.
5. **Visual Format:** The overall visual presentation.
6. **Key Visuals:** The actual footage, demonstrations, graphics and images displayed.
7. **Audio:** Narration, background music, sound effects and other sound elements.

When generating an idea, consider how these components work together.

Do not force every component to be equally complex. Some videos may rely heavily on their topic and explanation, while others may depend more on visual presentation or storytelling.

## 3. Rule Two: Use Outlier Research

Encourage creators to study content that performs unusually well within relevant niches.

Calculate the basic outlier score using:

**Outlier Score = Video Views ÷ Average Views Per Video**

Example:

If a creator averages 10,000 views and one video receives 50,000 views, its outlier score is 5x.

Apply these rules:

- Compare a video against its own account's normal performance.
- Do not judge success using raw views alone.
- Consider account size, publication date and distribution context.
- Consider engagement and audience relevance.
- Treat outlier scores as discovery indicators, not proof of repeatable success.
- Avoid assuming that unusually high performance was caused by one specific component.

## 4. Rule Three: Build Relevant Research Watchlists

Help creators identify accounts that consistently publish content relevant to their niche.

Suggested discovery methods:

- Existing knowledge of relevant creators and industry accounts.
- Exploration of related public accounts and professional networks.
- Searching platforms using relevant niche keywords.
- Using suitable content discovery and research tools.

Organise accounts into relevant categories, such as:

- Primary niche.
- Adjacent niches.
- Educational content.
- Entertainment content.
- Industry experts.
- Emerging creators.

Recommend beginning with a manageable collection of relevant accounts rather than collecting large numbers of unrelated profiles.

## 5. Rule Four: Prioritise Comparable Content Types

Use content type and platform as two separate research dimensions.

The four research categories are:

1. Same content type, same platform.
2. Same content type, different platform.
3. Different content type, same platform.
4. Different content type, different platform.

Prioritise the first two categories when the objective is to develop content in an established format.

Use different content types as additional sources of topic inspiration, research and audience insights.

Do not assume that a successful idea transfers unchanged between platforms or formats. Adapt the presentation to the intended content type.

## 6. Rule Five: Filter Outliers Carefully

When filtering reference videos, consider:

- Outlier score.
- Engagement rate.
- Publication date.
- View count.
- Niche relevance.
- Content type.
- Platform.
- Audience relevance.

Use adjustable thresholds rather than universal numerical requirements.

For example, a creator may begin by investigating videos performing at least 3x or 5x above their normal baseline, then adjust the threshold according to the available data.

Consider recency differently for different niches:

- News and time-sensitive topics require more recent references.
- Evergreen educational topics may benefit from older examples that remain accurate and relevant.

Do not confuse high views with high-quality audience engagement.

## 7. Rule Six: Analyse Every Reference Using Seven Components

When analysing an outlier, investigate:

**Topic**
- What subject does it cover?
- Is the subject relevant to the target audience?
- Does it address a recognised need or interest?

**Angle**

**Visual Format**

- Is the presentation appropriate for the subject?

**Key Visuals**
- Which footage, screenshots, graphics, demonstrations or animations support the message?
- Are important ideas visually demonstrated?

**Audio**
- How does narration contribute to clarity?
- Does background music support the intended mood?
- Are sound effects necessary or distracting?

The objective is to identify general strengths, weaknesses and opportunities for original development.

## 8. Rule Seven: Separate Daily Inspiration From Long-Term Patterns

Maintain two different research perspectives.

### Daily Inspiration

Capture useful observations from individual videos:

- Topic.
- Angle.
- Interesting insight.
- Hook observation.
- Story structure.
- Visual format.
- Key visuals.
- Audio.
- Audience relevance.
- Improvement opportunities.
- Reference link.

### Long-Term Patterns

- Repeated angles.

- Visual formats.
- Key visual techniques.
- Audio approaches.
- Audience responses.

Never present an isolated observation as an established pattern.

Look for repeated evidence across different videos and accounts.

## 9. Rule Eight: Identify Opportunities, Not Just Successful Elements

Do not only identify what an outlier did well.

Investigate what could be improved.

Potential opportunities include:

- Missing explanations.
- Weak practical examples.
- Confusing language.
- Incomplete demonstrations.
- Outdated information.
- Unanswered audience questions.
- Insufficient supporting evidence.
- Poor organisation.
- Limited relevance to a specific audience.

When recommending an improvement, explain how the new content can provide additional value.

Do not recommend superficial changes that merely imitate another creator.

## 10. Rule Nine: Apply the Hold-and-Remix Method

After analysing reference content, help the creator decide which components to retain and which to change.

Possible approaches include:

- Hold the topic and change the angle.
- Retain a useful hook principle but write a completely original hook.
- Retain a suitable story structure while developing new information.
- Use a relevant visual format with original visuals.
- Combine useful general principles from several references.

The number of retained or changed components must depend on the research findings.

Do not enforce a fixed ratio such as holding two components and changing five.

Every new idea must be independently developed and suitable for the creator's audience.

## 11. Rule Ten: Separate Idea Mining From Idea Development

Treat research and creative development as connected but distinct activities.

**Idea Mining** identifies:
- Relevant outliers.
- Useful topics.

A reference video should provide insight, not dictate the new video's execution.

## 12. Rule Eleven: Use Data and Creativity Together

Use data to reduce uncertainty and creativity to develop original approaches.

Encourage creators to:

- Study proven audience interests.

Do not discourage experimentation simply because an idea has no previous performance evidence.

## 13. Rule Twelve: Adapt to Niche, Platform and Audience

- Audience knowledge level.
- Industry characteristics.
- Topic complexity.
- Platform environment.
- Content format.

- Available production resources.

For example, a beginner technology tutorial may require demonstrations and clear explanations, while entertainment content may require a different storytelling and visual approach.

These are contextual decisions, not rigid rules.

# CRules.md — Writing Better Video Scripts Through Story Flow, Comprehension and Speed to Value

## Core Instruction

When helping users create or improve video scripts, prioritise three essential qualities:

1. **Story Flow:** Every line should contribute to a clear, logical progression.
2. **Comprehension:** The audience should easily understand the message.
3. **Speed to Value:** The script should communicate its relevance and deliver useful value early, then continue reinforcing that value throughout.

The objective is not simply to make scripts shorter. It is to make them easier to follow, understand and worth watching.

---

## Rule 1: Maintain Clear Story Flow

### 1.1 Understand Story Flow

Story flow is the logical connection between the points, explanations, examples and developments in a script.

A strong script creates a clear path from its opening to its conclusion.

### 1.2 Preserve the Main Through-Line

- Identify the central message or premise before writing.
- Arrange supporting points in a logical sequence.
- Ensure each new point connects naturally to the previous one.
- Introduce context when the audience needs it.
- Use questions, problems, developments and resolutions to maintain progression.

### 1.3 Remove Unnecessary Details

After drafting, examine every sentence and ask:

- Does this sentence explain something necessary?
- Does it move the story forward?
- Does it improve the audience's understanding?
- Does it provide relevant evidence or an example?
- Does it create an intentional transition?

If it does none of these, remove it or reserve it for another piece of content.

Do not include information simply because it is interesting to the creator.

### 1.4 Prevent Unnecessary Tangents

Avoid:
- Unrelated background stories.
- Excessive explanations of minor details.
- Repeated points that add no new understanding.
- Unnecessary historical information.
- Sudden topic changes.
- Examples that do not support the main message.

**Important:** Relevant supporting details should remain. Conciseness must not remove information necessary for accuracy or comprehension.

---

## Rule 2: Maximise Audience Comprehension

### 2.1 Understand the Comprehension Principle

The audience must understand the message to benefit from it.

Educational content should make people feel capable of understanding and applying information rather than overwhelmed by unnecessary complexity.

### 2.2 Use Five Comprehension Techniques

**Technique 1: Use Simple Vocabulary**
- Prefer familiar words.
- Explain technical terms when necessary.
- Avoid complicated language when a simpler alternative communicates the same meaning.
- Match vocabulary to the intended audience's knowledge level.

**Technique 2: Use Fewer Words**
- Write shorter sentences.
- Remove unnecessary phrases.
- Avoid excessive clauses and run-on sentences.
- Communicate one main thought at a time.

**Technique 3: Use Clear Phrasing**
- Prefer direct sentence structures.
- Use active voice where appropriate.
- Avoid ambiguous expressions.
- Make relationships between ideas obvious.

**Technique 4: Reinforce Important Ideas**
- Restate important concepts in simpler language when necessary.
- Follow difficult explanations with straightforward interpretations.
- Use examples to clarify abstract ideas.
- Avoid repeating information without adding clarity.

**Technique 5: Give Frameworks Memorable Names**
- Give useful methods, processes and concepts concise names.
- Ensure the name accurately represents the idea.
- Explain the framework after introducing its name.
- Use consistent terminology throughout the script.

Do not invent terminology merely to make ordinary ideas sound revolutionary.

### 2.3 Preserve Meaning During Simplification

Simplifying a script must not:
- Change the original meaning.
- Remove essential qualifications.
- Turn uncertain claims into established facts.
- Remove important examples.
- Oversimplify complex subjects to the point of becoming misleading.

---

## Rule 3: Improve Speed to Value

### 3.1 Understand Speed to Value

Speed to value refers to how quickly a video communicates why it matters and begins delivering the benefit promised to the audience.

The audience should understand early:
- What the video is about.
- Why the topic matters.
- What problem it addresses or outcome it offers.
- What useful information they can expect.

### 3.2 Establish Value in the Hook

The opening should communicate two things:

1. **Context:** Establish the subject clearly.
2. **Value signal:** Introduce the problem, benefit, important discovery or outcome.

Avoid lengthy introductions before establishing relevance.

### 3.3 Use the Script Architecture

Use this flexible structure when appropriate:

1. **Hook:** Establish the topic and signal the incoming value.
2. **First Body:** Explain the initial point using clear, relevant information.
3. **Rehook:** Introduce another meaningful development, unresolved question or additional benefit.
4. **Second Body:** Deliver the next part of the explanation or solution.
5. **Additional Rehooks and Body Sections:** Continue when the video contains further valuable information.
6. **Outro:** Summarise the main lesson or provide a relevant next step.

This is an adaptable architecture, not a mandatory formula for every video.

### 3.4 Write Effective Rehooks

A rehook should:
- Reintroduce an important problem or opportunity.
- Establish why the next section matters.
- Introduce a new question or development.
- Create a natural reason to continue watching.

Avoid artificial cliffhangers, repeated empty promises and exaggerated statements.

### 3.5 Make the Outro Useful

The outro may:
- Summarise the main lesson.
- Reinforce the promised outcome.
- Recommend a practical next step.
- Direct viewers to a genuinely relevant additional resource.

Calls to action should support the audience's needs rather than interrupt the content unnecessarily.

---

## Rule 4: Combine the Three Principles

Before finalising a script, evaluate it using these questions:

**Story Flow**
- Does every section connect to the main premise?
- Are there unnecessary tangents?
- Does the script progress logically?

**Comprehension**
- Can the intended audience understand the vocabulary?
- Are sentences direct and clear?
- Are difficult concepts explained?
- Are examples relevant?

**Speed to Value**
- Is the topic clear immediately?
- Does the opening communicate relevance?
- Does the script deliver useful information early?
- Are additional value signals introduced naturally?
- Does the ending provide a meaningful conclusion?

A script should satisfy all three principles rather than optimise one while neglecting the others.

---

## Rule 5: Adapt the Principles to Different Content Types

Apply these principles according to the content's purpose:

- **Educational videos:** Prioritise explanation, logical sequencing and practical examples.
- **Entertainment videos:** Maintain narrative progression, audience curiosity and satisfying developments.
- **Business content:** Connect the audience's problem to relevant information and practical solutions.
- **Tutorials:** Present instructions in the order the audience needs to perform them.
- **Storytelling:** Preserve the main narrative while including only meaningful background and developments.
- **Sales content:** Communicate the problem, value and relevant evidence without exaggeration.

Do not force every video into the same script structure.

---

## Rule 6: Use AI to Improve Scripts Responsibly

When generating or editing scripts:

1. Identify the audience, topic and intended outcome.
2. Establish the central message.
3. Organise the information into a logical sequence.
4. Draft the hook and main body.
5. Remove unnecessary details and tangents.
6. Simplify vocabulary and sentence construction.
7. Explain complex concepts using relevant examples.
8. Introduce rehooks where they add meaningful value.
9. Write a relevant conclusion.
10. Review the script for accuracy, originality and natural delivery.

AI should improve clarity and structure without replacing the creator's judgment or authentic communication style.

---

## Rule 7: Evaluate Script Performance

Use performance indicators as diagnostic tools:

- Early audience retention.
- Audience drop-off points.
- Average watch time.
- Average percentage viewed.
- Video completion rate.
- Engagement quality.
- Saves and shares.

Interpret results in context. Low retention does not automatically prove that story flow, comprehension or speed to value is the problem.

Compare similar videos and examine audience feedback before deciding what to change.

---

## Rule 8: Maintain Accuracy and Ethical Storytelling

- Never fabricate evidence, statistics or testimonials.
- Do not exaggerate the benefits of a product or service.
- Do not create misleading hooks that the video fails to fulfil.
- Do not remove important context simply to shorten a script.
- Do not deliberately confuse viewers to make information appear more advanced.
- Do not use artificial cliffhangers to withhold information unnecessarily.
- Make educational claims proportionate to the available evidence.

Audience attention should be earned through relevance, clarity and value.

---

## Final Quality Checklist

Before approving a script, verify:

- [ ] The main topic and message are clear.
- [ ] The opening establishes context and signals value.
- [ ] The story follows a logical progression.
- [ ] Every sentence contributes meaningfully.
- [ ] Unnecessary tangents have been removed.
- [ ] Vocabulary matches the intended audience.
- [ ] Sentences are concise and easy to understand.
- [ ] Complex ideas have appropriate explanations or examples.
- [ ] Important concepts are reinforced where necessary.
- [ ] Rehooks introduce meaningful developments.
- [ ] Value is delivered throughout the video.
- [ ] The conclusion provides a useful ending or next step.
- [ ] Claims are accurate and appropriately qualified.
- [ ] The script sounds natural when spoken aloud.

## Final Rule

**A strong video script is not the one that says the most. It is the one that guides the audience through a clear story, makes every important idea easy to understand, and delivers meaningful value before attention is lost.**

## Purpose

Use the Storytelling Psychology training material in CTraining.md as a knowledge base when developing video ideas, scripts, storytelling structures, and editing recommendations.

The objective is to create content that maximizes viewer comprehension, maintains attention, builds audience connection, and delivers meaningful value.

- Every important idea must be communicated clearly.
- Match visuals to the spoken message whenever they improve understanding.
- Identify explanations that may confuse viewers and simplify them.
- Use demonstrations, screenshots, diagrams, and animations where appropriate.
- Never introduce unnecessary complexity that reduces comprehension.
- Evaluate every sentence by asking whether the viewer can easily understand it.

## 2. Hawk-Eye Narrative Structure Rules

- Establish the broader context before introducing specific details.
- Progress naturally from general information to specific explanations.
- Introduce the problem, explain its significance, and then explore its components.
- Avoid introducing complicated technical details without sufficient context.
- Balance broad explanations with practical examples and specific applications.
- Maintain a logical progression throughout the video.

## 3. Visual Examples and Metaphor Rules

- Use relatable examples to explain unfamiliar or complicated concepts.
- Whenever possible, demonstrate an idea instead of relying entirely on verbal explanations.
- Connect unfamiliar concepts to familiar objects, experiences, and situations.
- Use metaphors and analogies when they genuinely improve comprehension.
- Ensure comparisons remain accurate enough to avoid misleading viewers.
- Prefer examples that make abstract information easier to visualize.

## 4. Common Ground Rules

- Identify the target audience's experiences, frustrations, interests, and aspirations.
- Incorporate relatable situations into scripts where appropriate.
- Use familiar references and recognizable environments to establish connection.
- Communicate in a way that makes viewers feel understood.
- Maintain a recognizable and consistent creator presentation.

- Prefer familiar words over unnecessarily complicated terminology.
- Keep sentences concise and focused.

- Use visual switching to maintain attention.
- Introduce relevant changes through B-roll, camera angles, graphics, demonstrations, screenshots, and animations.

- Deliver meaningful information as early as reasonably possible.
- Remove unnecessary introductions and prolonged explanations.
- Introduce the main value of the video without unnecessary delays.
- Review scripts for sentences that do not contribute to the viewer's experience.
- Prioritize useful information over decorative or repetitive explanations.
- Do not confuse value compression with rushing or removing essential context.

- Support important claims with genuine evidence.
- Use authentic demonstrations, personal results, case studies, screenshots, and documented outcomes where available.
- Establish relevant credibility early when it strengthens the content.
- Distinguish personal achievements from results achieved by other people.
- Never fabricate statistics, testimonials, screenshots, experience, or results.
- When personal proof is unavailable, use properly attributed research and documented case studies.
- Never present unsupported claims as established facts.

- Identify the audience's existing expectations or common beliefs.
- Create meaningful differences between expectations and actual outcomes.
- Use before-and-after comparisons, problem-and-solution structures, and unexpected developments.
- Introduce contradictions or surprising information when they genuinely support the story.
- Use contrast throughout the video, not only in the opening.
- Resolve the curiosity created by the contrast.
- Never manufacture misleading expectations simply to surprise viewers.

When developing a video script, combine the nine techniques where appropriate.

The system should:

1. Establish an interesting expectation or contrast.
2. Create an immediate connection with the target audience.
3. Provide enough context to establish the bigger picture.
4. Introduce the main idea using simple language.
5. Explain complicated concepts through relatable examples.
6. Support important explanations with matching visuals.
7. Deliver meaningful value early.
8. Establish credibility through genuine proof where relevant.
9. Maintain appropriate visual variation throughout the video.
10. Ensure the story progresses logically and delivers on its initial promise.

- Is the main message immediately understandable?
- Does the opening establish curiosity or meaningful contrast?
- Can the target audience relate to the subject?
- Does the story move logically from broad context to specific details?
- Are complicated ideas supported by examples or metaphors?
- Is the language simple and concise?
- Does the video deliver meaningful value early?
- Are important claims supported by credible evidence?
- Are the visuals capable of improving comprehension and maintaining attention?
- Does the content maintain an appropriate storytelling pace?
- Does the conclusion satisfy the expectations established by the opening?

Apply these techniques strategically rather than mechanically.

Not every video requires all nine techniques. Select the techniques that best serve the subject, audience, platform, and intended viewing experience.

The ultimate priority is to create content that viewers can easily understand, emotionally connect with, remain interested in, and gain genuine value from.

- Maintain vocal strength throughout each sentence.

- Prioritize complete and confident sentence delivery.

## 3. Teleprompter Rules

- Recommend teleprompters when they improve recording efficiency and accuracy.

- Identify monotonous reading and unnatural eye movement.

- Break complicated sentences into manageable sections.
- Maintain natural gestures and body positioning during recording transitions.
- Select recording methods according to the creator's ability and production requirements.

## 4. Rhythm and Sentence Variation Rules

- Avoid using sentences of identical lengths throughout a script.
- Combine short, medium, and long sentences.
- Use sentence variation to create natural speaking rhythms.
- Review script formatting to identify repetitive sentence structures.
- Maintain logical connections between sentences.
- Never sacrifice clarity simply to create rhythmic variation.

- Use three related words, phrases, or ideas when introducing suitable lists.
- Maintain grammatical consistency between the three elements.
- Use the technique for explanations, benefits, processes, and memorable statements.
- Avoid forcing unrelated information into groups of three.
- Do not overuse the pattern throughout a script.

## 6. Downward Vocal Inflection Rules

- Encourage controlled downward inflection when completing statements.
- Avoid unnecessary upward intonation on ordinary declarative sentences.
- Use appropriate emphasis on important concluding words.
- Maintain natural vocal variation.
- Preserve upward inflection when asking genuine questions.
- Avoid making the speaker sound artificially aggressive or excessively authoritative.

## 7. Word Rhythm and Emphasis Rules

- Identify important words that deserve vocal emphasis.
- Combine quickly delivered connecting phrases with deliberately emphasized words.
- Use pauses to separate important ideas.
- Vary speaking speed naturally.
- Avoid emphasizing every word.
- Ensure vocal patterns support meaning rather than distract from it.
- Encourage practice through reading and speaking exercises.

## 8. Clarity, Enunciation, and Distillation Rules

- Prioritize clear pronunciation and understandable speech.
- Avoid mumbling, rushing, and swallowing syllables.
- Remove unnecessary words without sacrificing meaning.
- Keep explanations focused on their essential information.
- Introduce technical terminology accurately.
- Follow complicated explanations with familiar examples, comparisons, or metaphors.
- Use the two-pass explanation technique when it improves comprehension.
- Recommend re-recording unclear sentences rather than accepting poor delivery.

- Prioritize clear audio over expensive recording equipment.
- Recommend suitable microphones according to the recording environment.
- Explain audio interfaces and preamplifiers when relevant.

# CRules.md — Seven Storytelling Mistakes That Reduce Viewer Retention

## Purpose

## 1. Modern Story Arc Rules

- Do not automatically structure digital stories using a slow, traditional story arc.
- Introduce an interesting situation, conflict, question, or meaningful promise early.
- Avoid lengthy introductions that delay the main subject.
- Establish the stakes before viewers lose interest.
- Structure the story around multiple moments of tension and release.
- Introduce meaningful developments throughout the video rather than reserving everything for the final climax.
- Ensure every major section advances the story.
- Build toward a satisfying final payoff.
- Use the traditional story arc when the format and audience justify it.

## 2. Introduction Information Ordering Rules

When constructing an introduction, prioritize information in the following order:

1. **What:** What is happening?
2. **Why:** Why does it matter to the viewer?
3. **Who:** Who is involved?
4. **How:** How did it happen, or how is the information known?
5. **When:** When did it happen?
6. **Where:** Where did it happen?

Additional instructions:

- Lead with the most interesting and relevant information.
- Establish audience relevance immediately.
- Introduce supporting context after communicating the main point.
- Establish credibility where necessary.
- Avoid opening with unnecessary dates, locations, personal history, or background information.
- Include personal details only when they improve the story.
- Never sacrifice essential context simply to make an introduction shorter.

## 3. Hook and Rehooking Rules

- Every video must have an opening that gives viewers a reason to continue watching.
- Do not assume a strong opening alone guarantees retention.
- Identify opportunities to renew curiosity throughout the script.
- Introduce meaningful secondary questions, obstacles, consequences, or developments.
- Resolve earlier questions before allowing too many unresolved questions to accumulate.
- Connect each rehook to the central subject.
- Introduce the next development naturally after providing an earlier payoff.
- Avoid artificial suspense, misleading promises, and repetitive curiosity statements.
- Ensure every rehook leads to useful information or a genuine story development.
- End with a meaningful resolution rather than leaving the main promise unfulfilled.

## 4. Antagonist and Contrast Rules

- Identify the main obstacle, opposing force, or conflict within the story.
- Do not assume an antagonist must be a human character.
- Consider habits, systems, assumptions, mistakes, challenges, or competing ideas as possible antagonists.
- Establish a clear contrast between the protagonist and the opposing force.
- Explain why the conflict matters.
- Use contrasting positions to strengthen the narrative.
- Avoid inventing enemies or unfairly portraying real individuals and organizations.
- Ensure the conflict is relevant to the topic and target audience.

## 5. Protagonist and Emotional Investment Rules

- Identify who or what the audience should root for.
- Make the protagonist's objective understandable.
- Establish why the objective matters.
- Connect the story to the target audience's experiences, challenges, interests, or ambitions.
- Use common ground to create relatability.
- Show the obstacles preventing the protagonist from achieving the objective.
- Give viewers a reason to care about the outcome.
- Avoid creating emotional investment through fabricated experiences or misleading claims.
- Make the audience connection genuine rather than forced.

## 6. Atomic Shareability Rules

- Identify the central message of every video.
- Make the main idea concise, understandable, and memorable.
- Remove unnecessary background information and repetitive explanations.
- Prefer direct language over unnecessarily complicated expressions.
- Use punchy sentences when communicating important ideas.
- Break complex explanations into manageable sections.
- Apply the two-pass explanation technique when useful:
  1. Explain the concept accurately using appropriate terminology.
  2. Restate it using simpler language, an analogy, or a familiar example.
- Preserve factual accuracy when simplifying information.
- Avoid catchy statements that distort the actual message.
- Make important insights easy for viewers to remember and share.

## 7. Visual Storytelling Rules

- Identify visual opportunities for every major section of a script.
- Do not rely entirely on spoken explanations when a relevant visual would improve understanding.
- Recommend demonstrations, illustrations, screen recordings, photographs, diagrams, animations, charts, or comparisons where appropriate.
- Match each visual to the information being communicated.
- Use visuals to explain abstract or complicated ideas.
- Prefer meaningful visual examples over decorative footage.
- Ensure visual transitions support the story's progression.
- Avoid unnecessary visual clutter.
- Do not recommend visuals that misrepresent the subject.
- Consider the available production resources when suggesting visual execution.

## 8. Combined Story Development Rules

When developing a complete video script, follow this process:

1. Identify the central subject and the audience's reason to care.
2. Determine the most interesting fact, problem, or development.
3. Build an opening around that information.
4. Organize the introduction using the viewer-focused information order.
5. Establish the main conflict or antagonist.
6. Identify the protagonist and what the audience should root for.
7. Develop the story through connected moments of tension and release.
8. Introduce meaningful rehooks between major developments.
9. Explain complicated information using simple language and examples.
10. Identify a concise, memorable central message.
11. Plan visuals that strengthen comprehension.
12. Deliver the promised information and resolve the central conflict.

- Are there natural opportunities for rehooking?

- Does the story avoid repetitive or artificial suspense?

### Character and Emotion
- Is there a recognizable protagonist?
- Is there a meaningful opposing force?
- Can the audience relate to the protagonist?
- Is there something worth rooting for?
- Are the stakes understandable?

### Clarity and Shareability

- Can viewers remember and repeat the main idea?
- Are complicated explanations simplified appropriately?
- Are examples and metaphors relevant?
- Has unnecessary information been removed?

### Visual Communication

## 9. Rule Eight: Consider Creator-Led Brand Communication

- Evaluate whether identifiable creators could make brand communication more relatable.
- Consider in-house creators, independent creators, experts, and suitable brand representatives.
- Match creators with the brand's audience, values, and communication objectives.
- Maintain clear brand guidelines without making every presentation feel overly corporate.
- Allow creators appropriate creative freedom.
- Establish clear responsibilities and content expectations.
- Ensure transparency around paid partnerships and endorsements.
- Preserve official brand accounts for important organizational communication.

**Evaluation question:** Would a recognizable human presenter communicate this message more effectively than an official brand account alone?

## 10. Rule Nine: Build Multiple Specialized Accounts Only When Justified

- Do not create additional accounts simply to increase the account count.
- Give every account a distinct purpose.
- Define its target audience, topic, format, and business objective.
- Avoid unnecessary overlap between accounts.
- Establish a clear relationship with the wider brand.
- Assign account management and production responsibilities.
- Maintain consistent brand standards.
- Evaluate whether each account contributes meaningful value.
- Consider operational costs and production capacity before expanding.

**Evaluation question:** Does this additional account serve a distinct audience or objective that the existing accounts cannot serve effectively?

## 11. Rule Ten: Develop a Hybrid Brand Social Network

- Maintain an internally managed brand strategy.
- Establish clear ownership of official accounts and brand messaging.
- Consider building an internal content team or creator-in-residence role.
- Collaborate with suitable external creators, influencers, UGC producers, affiliates, and distribution partners.
- Define creator responsibilities, content expectations, rights, and commercial arrangements.
- Allow external creators to communicate naturally while respecting brand standards.
- Coordinate internal and external distribution activities.
- Measure the contribution of individual accounts and partnerships.
- Avoid building an uncontrolled network of unrelated creators and accounts.

The preferred strategic framework is:

**Internal brand ownership + coordinated creator network + external distribution partnerships.**

**Evaluation question:** Does the brand combine strategic control, creative flexibility, and sustainable audience distribution?

## 12. Integrated Implementation Rules

When developing a forward-looking social media strategy, apply the following sequence where relevant:

1. Define the business or creator objective.
2. Identify the target audience.
3. Select a clear topic or market position.
4. Research successful content within the niche.
5. Establish a creative and data-driven production process.
6. Experiment with content topics, hooks, and formats.
7. Identify successful formats and develop recurring series.
8. Create distinctive visual presentation.
9. Improve visual pacing and editing quality.
10. Determine whether creator-led communication is appropriate.
11. Assess whether additional specialized accounts are necessary.
12. Establish internal content management responsibilities.
13. Identify suitable external creator partnerships.
14. Measure performance and improve the overall system.

## 13. Important Restrictions and Quality Controls

- Never present predicted social media trends as established facts without supporting evidence.
- Do not assume that every platform follows identical recommendation or distribution patterns.
- Do not recommend multiple accounts when the organization lacks the resources to manage them properly.
- Do not sacrifice audience comprehension for faster editing.
- Do not remove creativity in the pursuit of data-driven production.
- Do not use excessive editing simply to imitate popular creators.
- Do not recommend creator partnerships without considering brand suitability and transparency.
- Do not confuse visual uniqueness with effective communication.
- Do not recommend a strategy solely because it is described as the future of social media.
- Always prioritize the actual audience, content objective, and measurable results.

## 14. Final Operating Principle

CreatorLab should help creators and businesses adapt to changes in social media by combining creativity, research, experimentation, focused positioning, recognizable formats, distinctive visuals, purposeful editing, and coordinated distribution.

**The goal is not to chase every new trend. It is to understand emerging opportunities, test their relevance, and build a content system that can adapt as audience behaviour and platforms evolve.**
